Contemporary romance by Indian authors has a huge, thriving catalog. Fantasy and romantasy from Indian authors is smaller, but what&apos;s there is genuinely worth seeking out. Here are three standouts.

## 1. [The Jasmine Throne](/books/the-jasmine-throne) by Tasha Suri

<BookCover slug="the-jasmine-throne" />

An imprisoned princess and a rebel priestess forge an unlikely alliance in an Indian-inspired epic fantasy where politics, magic, and a slow-building romance are inseparable from each other.

**Why it works:** Suri builds the romance out of shared danger and mutual respect rather than instant attraction, and the alliance-turned-something-more payoff is worth the patience.

## 2. [SAMSARA](/books/samsara-saksham-garg-mnjbnld2) by Saksham Garg

<BookCover slug="samsara-saksham-garg-mnjbnld2" />

An epic journey through the hidden realms of Indian mythology and the cycle of rebirth, for readers who want their fantasy romance grounded in a mythological framework rather than a generic Western one.

**Why it works:** Leaning directly into the cosmology of samsara and rebirth gives the story stakes that feel genuinely different from the usual fantasy quest structure.

## 3. [Once Upon a Curfew](/books/once-upon-a-curfew) by Srishti Chaudhary

<BookCover slug="once-upon-a-curfew" />

Set during the Indian Emergency, this follows two sisters who open a library and find love in the middle of real political turmoil. Less traditional fantasy, more historical romance with the kind of high stakes fantasy readers will still recognize.

**Why it works:** Grounding the romance in a genuine, high-tension moment in Indian history gives it a weight that pure invented fantasy worlds can&apos;t always match.
